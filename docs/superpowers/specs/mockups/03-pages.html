12.4 Blood pressure

Blood pressure is the force exerted by circulating blood on the walls of the arteries, measured in millimetres of mercury.

Normal adult blood pressure is defined as a systolic pressure below 120 mmHg and a diastolic pressure below 80 mmHg. Readings vary with age, activity, posture and stress.

1

The difference between systolic and diastolic pressure is called the pulse pressure. It reflects the volume of blood ejected with each beat.

A cuff that is too small gives a falsely high reading. Choose a cuff whose bladder covers about 80% of the arm.

The cardiac cycle
  • Systole: ventricles contract and eject blood
  • Diastole: ventricles relax and fill
  • One cycle lasts about 0.8 s at 75 beats per minute

About your selection on p. 112: "The difference between systolic and diastolic pressure is called the pulse pressure."
Explain this more simply

Take the top number and subtract the bottom number. For 120/80 the pulse pressure is 40. It shows how hard each heartbeat pushes.1

About slide 14: The cardiac cycle
Explain the diagram

Systole is when the ventricles squeeze and push blood out. Diastole is when they relax and fill again. The two repeat as one cycle.1
